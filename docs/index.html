03 / Check the algebra, keep the limits

Descriptive PCA, no economic identification

Diagnostic Value

Changes, not levels.

Six tenors: 0.25, 1, 2, 5, 10 and 30 years. Generate 96 deterministic changes using cosine-shaped directions, correlated time patterns and a small fixed drift. The amplitude controls affect generating shapes, not fitted PC variances. The fitted directions can mix those shapes.

Illustrative starting yields are 3 + 0.2 log(1 + tenor) percent. Add each change / 100 to obtain the next yield in percent. Levels are audit context only. No pricing, cash flows, duration, discount factors, arbitrage-free construction or market calibration.

Fit, then freeze.

Subtract each tenor's mean from the first 48 or 72 changes. Sample covariance C = XᵀX / (n−1). Largest-pivot Jacobi rotations find all six eigenvectors. Sort eigenvalues descending; signs are fixed only for reproducibility.

Project any row onto the fitted vectors and retain k components. Keep the same training mean and vectors for later rows. Held-out scores still require the contemporaneous observed change. Their cross-covariances need not vanish outside training.

Variance is not certainty.

Fit variance captured = sum retained eigenvalues / covariance trace. RMSE = sqrt(sum squared reconstruction residuals / (rows × 6)). Training SSE equals (n−1) × sum discarded eigenvalues, up to floating-point rounding.

PCA makes training scores uncorrelated, not independent or causal. It minimizes squared training reconstruction error at fixed dimension and scaling; it does not guarantee stable factors, Gaussian changes, a hedge, an economic interpretation or future performance. No confidence intervals, forecast or investment advice.

Background: MIT: yield-change PCA · CMU: dimension reduction · Jacobi rotation method. No external code, data, fonts or assets bundled.
